Finding the right person

The hard part is choosing. We made it easier.

The right trainer for a nervous beginner looks different from the right trainer for a marathoner. Each of these capabilities takes some of the guesswork out of choosing.

Matchmaking

A shortlist that explains itself

Eight or so questions about your goals, experience, schedule, budget, and the kind of coaching you like. You get ranked trainers with a plain reason for each: "Specializes in beginners, free Tuesday and Thursday evenings, 1.2 miles away, $60."

Prices and availability up front

No "DM for rates"

Every profile shows what sessions cost and when the trainer is actually free. You never have to start an awkward conversation to find out whether you can afford someone.

Comfort filters

Find someone you'll feel at ease with

Filter by trainer gender, language, and coaching approach, and by experience with seniors, adaptive athletes, neurodivergent clients, LGBTQ+ affirming, or body-positive coaching (self-declared and labeled that way).

Free consults

Meet before you commit

Trainers can offer a free 15-minute call or meeting. Book it like any session, ask your questions, and decide afterward.

Verified reviews and badges

Trust what you read

Verified reviews come only from people who completed a session. Certifications, liability insurance, and CPR are checked and shown as badges.

Where you train

In a gym, at home, outside, or online

Trainers list whether they train in a gym, in your home, outdoors, or over video. Not ready for a gym floor? Start somewhere you're comfortable.

What to expect

Your first month, step by step

Starting is the hardest part, so here is roughly how the first few weeks go. Your trainer will shape the details around you.

  1. 1Day 1

    Take the quiz, pick a shortlist

    Five minutes of questions. Save the two or three trainers that feel right, compare them side by side, and book a free consult.

  2. 2Before the first session

    Health questionnaire, in plain language

    A standard health screen with an assistant that explains the questions. It goes only to the trainer you book, who sees a summary and any items that need a doctor's OK.

  3. 3Week 1

    First session

    Your trainer learns how you move and what you enjoy. Pay by card, Apple Pay, or Google Pay, or buy a package to save per session.

  4. 4Weeks 2 to 4

    A plan for the days in between

    Short workouts and simple habits on your phone, like a daily step goal or a protein target. Log them as you go, and your trainer checks in weekly.

  5. 5End of the month

    See how far you've come

    Completion, streaks, measurements, and goal progress on a chart you and your trainer both see. Then you plan the next month together.

Between sessions

Most of your progress happens when your trainer isn't there

An hour or two a week with a trainer is the start. What you do on the other days decides the result, and Trainerdate makes those days easier to get right.

Your plan on your phone

Always know what to do today

Today's workout with demo videos, a rest timer, and one-tap logging. If the machine is taken, swap to an alternative your trainer approved.

Between-sessions assistant

Answers at 9 pm, from your trainer's plan

"What weight should I use?" "How do I do this one?" With a Pro trainer, an assistant answers from their program. Anything about pain or injury goes straight to your trainer.

Progress tracking

Proof that it's working

Streaks, habits, measurements, strength gains, and goal progress on charts. On the weeks the scale doesn't move, you can still see what did.

Safe payments

Pay without the worry

Receipts, refunds according to a policy you saw before booking, and dispute support. Packages lower the per-session price once you've found your person.

Beginner guides

Know what you're walking into

Library guides explain what a yoga class, a BJJ fundamentals class, or a first strength session is really like, so trying something new is less of a leap.

Community

People cheering you on

Join your trainer's community, local forums, and challenges. Share a milestone when you want to, and keep everything private when you don't.

Questions

Questions from people just starting

I'm out of shape and a bit embarrassed. Is this for me?

Yes. Trainers who specialize in beginners say so on their profiles, and matchmaking asks what kind of coaching you're comfortable with. Training at home or online is an option if a gym isn't.

How much does a trainer cost?

Trainers set their own prices, and every profile shows them. Filter by your budget, look for free consults and intro offers, and use packages to bring the per-session price down. Trainerdate itself is free for you.

Do I need to see a doctor first?

The health questionnaire uses standard screening questions, and if anything suggests you should get medical clearance, it tells you and your trainer. Trainerdate doesn't give medical advice. If you have a health condition, talk to your doctor before starting a new exercise program.

Who sees my health information?

Only trainers you book. Your profile and progress are private by default, and you choose what to share.

What if it doesn't work out with my trainer?

Cancel within their policy (shown before you booked), and go back to your shortlist. Your workout history and progress stay with you.
