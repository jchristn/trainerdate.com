How it works

From "I should get a trainer" to a first session

Most people give up on finding a trainer somewhere between an unanswered DM and a "contact us for pricing" page. Trainerdate puts the answers on the profile.

  1. 1

    Search the map

    Filter by specialty, price, distance, schedule, language, and coaching style. Or type it the way you'd say it: "female trainer near Capitol Hill who does postpartum, under $90."

  2. 2

    Compare with real information

    Profiles show prices, open times, checked credentials, and reviews from completed sessions. You know what a session costs before you message anyone.

  3. 3

    Book and pay

    Pick a slot shown in your time zone, pay by card, Apple Pay, or Google Pay, or use a package credit. Reminders and a calendar invite come with it.

  4. 4

    Keep going between sessions

    Your program lives on your phone with a rest timer and logging. Your trainer sees what you did and adjusts the next week.

What the platform does

Every feature answers a real complaint

Trainers juggle four apps and a Venmo account. Clients can't tell a good trainer from a good Instagram. Gyms are invisible unless they pay for ads. Here is what Trainerdate does about each of those.

Map discovery

Every gym, studio, and academy near you, on one map

Trainerdate maps gyms, yoga studios, BJJ and MMA academies, and functional fitness boxes before launch, so the map is full on the first day in each city instead of waiting for businesses to sign up.

Matchmaking

A shortlist with the reasons written out

A short quiz about your goals, schedule, budget, and the kind of coaching you respond to returns ranked trainers, each with a plain explanation of why they fit. Sponsored listings never enter the results.

Verified reviews and credentials

Reviews and badges you can believe

A review counts as verified only after a completed booking. Certifications, liability insurance, and CPR are checked by staff and shown as badges, with reminders before they expire.

Scheduling

Book in three taps, rebook in one

Open slots come straight from the trainer's calendar. Instant booking or request-to-book, waitlists that refill cancelled slots, recurring sessions, and reminders on both sides.

Payments

The money part, handled

Cards on file, deposits, session packages, monthly memberships, tips, and receipts, all through Stripe. Trainers are paid out in about two business days. Clients see the refund policy before they book.

Programs and progress

A plan for the days you train alone

Trainers assign programs, habits, and weekly check-ins built from a reviewed exercise catalog. Clients log sets on their phone, and both of them watch PRs, trends, and goal progress on the same charts.

Messaging

Conversations that stay organized

One-on-one and group chat with workout cards, booking proposals, payment requests, voice notes, and form-check videos. First messages from strangers are screened for spam and scams.

Communities

A paid community without building a platform

Any coach or gym can run a branded space with a feed, forums, a video lesson library, events with tickets, and paid membership tiers. BJJ with Joel runs on this same model today.

AI assistance

More coaching out of every hour

AI drafts programs, sums up each client's week before a session, and answers routine questions from the trainer's own plan. Nothing reaches a client until the trainer approves it.

AI with a human in charge

AI does the busywork. The trainer makes the calls.

Trainers are good at coaching and short on hours. Trainerdate uses AI for the typing, summarizing, and answering of routine questions that eat those hours, and keeps every training decision with the person who has a certification and knows the client.

The trainer approves everything

Program drafts, profile copy, and posts stay drafts until the trainer signs off. Clients only ever see their trainer's plan.

Grounded in real records

Client summaries link back to the logs, check-ins, and notes they came from. The between-sessions assistant answers from the trainer's program and the exercise catalog.

It knows when to hand off

Pain, injury, medical questions, and requests to change the plan go straight to the trainer as a message. The assistant doesn't diagnose and doesn't improvise.

No invented exercises

Drafts use only movements from the reviewed catalog, and health-screening red flags block a draft until the trainer has read them.

Trust and safety

You're meeting a stranger to exercise. That deserves care.

Training is personal and sometimes happens in your home. Trust is designed into Trainerdate from the first search.

Honest reviews

Only real clients get the verified label

Verified reviews require a completed session. Anything else is clearly labeled. Review summaries on profiles include the negative themes along with the praise, with links to the reviews behind them.

Protected payments

No cash-app risk

Payments made on the platform come with receipts, refunds under a policy you saw before booking, and dispute support that pulls the booking record, check-in time, and messages together automatically.

Privacy by default

Your health details stay yours

Trainee profiles are private unless you choose otherwise. Health questionnaires are shared only with trainers you book. Individual trainers never get a public page without opting in.

Safer conversations

Screened first messages, block and report everywhere

First messages are screened for scams and harassment. Every profile, post, and conversation can be blocked or reported, and human moderators work the queue.

The story

The original came out in 2011. This one runs the business.

The first Trainerdate was an iOS and Android directory for finding a trainer. Directories lost to Google Maps and Instagram, and the lesson stuck: a listing alone doesn't keep a trainer around.

The new Trainerdate handles the work a trainer does after a client finds them: the calendar, the payments, the programs, the check-ins. AI fills the map before launch, so a new city opens with gyms and studios already on it. Trainerdate is run by Lattice AI, LLC, the team behind BJJ with Joel.

Questions

Common questions

Is Trainerdate free for people looking for a trainer?

Yes. Searching, booking, messaging, logging workouts, and tracking progress cost nothing. You pay your trainer for sessions, and Trainerdate adds no subscription on top.

When can I use it?

Trainerdate is in private alpha now. The founding beta opens in California, Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Nevada, Arizona, Hawaii, and Alaska. Trainers and gyms come first so the map is full when trainees arrive. Request early access below and we'll tell you when your area opens.

What kinds of training are on it?

Launch disciplines are personal training, BJJ, MMA, yoga, and CrossFit-style training. Pilates, boxing, Muay Thai, kettlebell, mobility, running, pre and postnatal, senior fitness, and the rest of the modality list follow.

Is there an app?

One app for iOS and Android that adapts to your role, so a trainer who also trains with someone else doesn't need two apps. Everything also works on the web. App Store and Google Play links appear here at launch.

Does the AI replace my trainer?

No. It drafts programs for your trainer to edit, summarizes your progress for them, and answers routine questions from their plan. Your trainer approves every program and can see every conversation, and anything about pain or injury goes straight to them.

How does Trainerdate make money?

Trainers and gyms subscribe for tools (Pro, Studio, and the Creator add-on for paid communities), and Trainer Free carries a 5% platform fee on payments. We don't sell leads. Any sponsored placement is labeled and kept out of matchmaking.
